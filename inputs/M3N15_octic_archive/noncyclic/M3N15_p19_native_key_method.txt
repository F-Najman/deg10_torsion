Exact source coverage without the Magma residue-field cache failure

For a degree-d closed point of X1(3,15), its canonical coordinates q,t
generate its residue field: they give the specified universal E,P3,Q15,
and separate its noncuspidal moduli points. For d=2,3,4 the proper
subfields of F_(p^d) are nested. Hence q or t generates F_(p^d).
Choose q if it does; otherwise choose t. A canonical key consists of
this choice, the monic minimum polynomial of the chosen coordinate,
and the coefficients of the other coordinate in its power basis.
It determines the ordered pair up to Frobenius, and is unchanged by
changing finite-field representation. Both assertions follow by
sending the chosen primitive element to each root of its minimum
polynomial. The code checks the power-basis reconstruction exactly.

The fourth-prime source has 32076 quartic places. Native function-field
enumeration is fast, but converting/evaluating all places in a single
Magma process fills its global residue-field cache. Six fresh bounded
processes each enumerate the whole native source and export a disjoint
index interval of actual q,t keys. The aggregate is accepted only if
all 32076 keys are distinct. Since every key is an actual place and
the total agrees with the independently verified Frobenius count,
this proves complete coverage even if place ordering differs between
processes. No cross-run index identity is used.

The next stage uses one fixed finite field. It reconstructs only each
new orbit representative from its key, applies all 48 modular
automorphisms, and checks every resulting key belongs to the complete
source. Distinct orbits are checked disjoint and their union complete.
Only representative places are then reconstructed by the common-zero
inverse. Their exact degree and original key are checked individually.
The first whole orbit is independently compared with the original
modular inverse. The resulting arithmetic stage reduces only the
first support; all second supports and cusp complements remain in it.

Failed probes and bounded memory-limit logs are preserved. They supply
no exclusion evidence. The accepted source must have all completion,
key-coverage, original-inverse and arithmetic replay assertions pass.
