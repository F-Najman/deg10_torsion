\begin{proposition}\label{prop:nonic-no961}
An elliptic curve over a number field of degree $9$ has no point of order $961$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The complete winding computation for $J_1(961)$ has total dimension
$37751=g(X_1(961))$. All character components have full winding submodule
except possibly characters with orders in
$\{1\}$.
The diamond $\langle3\rangle$ has projective order $465$, coprime
to every nontrivial exceptional character order. Thus each exceptional
character is trivial at $3$, and Kato's theorem gives that
$(\langle3\rangle-1)J_1(961)(\mathbb Q)$ is finite.
Full-rank $T_2$ Krylov matrices modulo splitting primes certify several
high-order character components. The remaining components are computed
using ordinary winding submodules. Every coefficient denominator in a
modular certificate is invertible at the chosen prime, so full rank
in that certificate holds in characteristic zero.
The complete dimension sum, the character evaluations, and every serialized
Krylov matrix are checked independently in the accompanying certificate.

Apply \cite[Proposition~4.4]{DN} at $q=3$, with $k_3=7$.
The exact modular index is $I_{961}=461280$, and
\[
 9k_3=63<\frac{325\cdot461280}{32768}
       \leq\operatorname{gon}_{\mathbb C}X_1(961).
\]
The least degree of a CM point is $310>9$.
The trace divisor of a hypothetical degree-nine torsion point therefore
has non-CM support and is stable under this diamond.
Its free action would force $465\mid9$, a contradiction.
\end{proof}
