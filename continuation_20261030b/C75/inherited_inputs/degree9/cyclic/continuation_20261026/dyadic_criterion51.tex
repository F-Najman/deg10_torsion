\subsection{A dyadic formal-immersion criterion}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:nonic51-dyadic-Hecke}
Let $A/\Z_2$ be an abelian scheme, and let $T,\delta$ be
endomorphisms satisfying $T_{\F_2}=F+\delta_{\F_2}V$.
Then $T-\delta$ kills every point in $A(\Q_2)[2]$ reducing to zero.
For an odd-level modular Jacobian, it therefore kills the torsion
kernel of reduction.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The schematic closure of a nonzero such point and zero in $A[2]$
is finite flat of rank two with connected special fibre. By the
rank-two case of Tate--Oort \cite{TO}, it is $\mu_2$.
Indeed the parameters $a,c\in\Z_2$ satisfy $ac=2$, and connected
special fibre forces $a\in2\Z_2$, so $c$ is a unit.
For its inclusion $\iota:\mu_2\hookrightarrow A[2]$, naturality
and $F_{\mu_2}=0$, $V_{\mu_2}=1$ give
$(T-\delta)\overline\iota=0$.
Reduction on $\operatorname{Hom}_{\Z_2}(\mu_2,A[2])$ is injective:
by Cartier duality this is the assertion for maps from $A^\vee[2]$
to the constant etale group $\Z/2\Z$. Such a map is determined by
its open and closed fibres, which are determined by their special
fibres over the henselian ring $\Z_2$.
Thus $(T-\delta)\iota=0$.
For the last assertion, Parent's reduction-kernel lemma, as used
in \cite[Section~5]{DN}, gives exponent two, and Eichler--Shimura
gives $T_2=F+\langle2\rangle V$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:nonic51-quotient-Cartier}
Let $Y/\Q$ be a modular curve with good reduction at $2$ and a
rational cusp $c_0$. Suppose that its Jacobian $J$ has rank zero
over $\Q$, that its cusp scheme is etale at $2$ with cyclotomic
fields of definition, and that $T_2,\delta=\langle2\rangle$ descend
to $J$ and satisfy Eichler--Shimura at $2$.
Put $U=(\mathcal C-1)H^0(Y_{\F_2},\Omega^1)$, where $\mathcal C$
is Cartier. If restriction from $U$ to every effective cuspidal
divisor $\overline C$ of degree $d$ is surjective, then no
noncuspidal rational effective divisor of degree $d$ on $Y$
has entirely cuspidal reduction at $2$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Suppose $D$ is such a divisor. The unique cuspidal lift $C$ of
$\overline D$ is fixed by Frobenius at $2$. It is defined over a
decomposition field $L$ in a suitable cyclotomic field, with
$L_\lambda=\Q_2$. The classes $[D-dc_0]$ and $[C-dc_0]$ are
torsion, respectively by rank zero and Manin--Drinfeld.
Thus $[D-C]$ is torsion and reduces to zero. Parent's lemma and
Lemma~\ref{lem:nonic51-dyadic-Hecke} give
$(T_2-\delta)[D-C]=0$.
This uses rank over $\Q$ only, not rank over $L$.

Consider $f:Y^{(d)}\to J$, $E\mapsto(T_2-\delta)[E-dc_0]$.
In characteristic two its cotangent image is
$\delta^*(\mathcal C-1)H^0(\Omega^1)=U$.
Here Frobenius pulls back to zero, Verschiebung pulls back to
Cartier, and the invertible operator $\delta$ commutes with Cartier.
The stated restriction condition is exactly formal immersion at
$\overline C$, including for nonreduced $\overline C$.
The two $\Z_2$-sections $D,C$ have equal reductions and equal
images under $f$, so formal immersion implies $D=C$, a contradiction.
\end{proof}

The restriction condition can be checked without power series.
In a basis of $H^0(\Omega^1)$, compute the kernels
$H^0(\Omega^1(-mP))$ for every cusp $P$ and every
$m\leq d/\deg(P)$. Their annihilators give compatible restriction
matrices. Concatenating the blocks at different cusps gives the
restriction at $\sum m_P P$. Its rank on $U$ must be $d$.
The certificates below export these kernels or the original-basis
jets, the Cartier matrix, the basis of $U$, and every restriction
matrix. Independent binary linear algebra checks both the
projections and exhaustive weighted enumeration.
