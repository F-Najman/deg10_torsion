\begin{lemma}
Let $e\mid d$ and let $G\in\Phi(e)$. Then $G\in\Phi(d)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix $E/F$ with $[F:\mathbb Q]=e$ and $E(F)_{\rm tors}=G$.
By uniform boundedness in degree $d$, only finitely many torsion points of
$E(\overline F)$ can become rational over an extension of $F$ of degree
$d/e$. For each such point outside $E(F)$ its field of definition is a
nontrivial finite extension of $F$. Hilbert irreducibility supplies a
degree-$d/e$ extension $K/F$ linearly disjoint from the compositum of
these finitely many fields. Then $E(K)_{\rm tors}=G$.
\end{proof}

The complete quintic classification gives the $35$ groups
$C_n$ for $1\le n\le25$, $n\ne23$, and $n=28,30$, together with
$C_2\times C_{2n}$ for $1\le n\le9$.
They all occur in degree $10$ by the lemma. The quadratic groups
$C_3\times C_3$, $C_3\times C_6$ and $C_4\times C_4$ give three more.
This establishes $38$ exact full torsion groups, before any fresh
construction. The other quadratic groups are already in this list.
