\begin{proposition}\label{prop:octic-no105}
An elliptic curve over a number field of degree $8$ has no point of
order $105$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The exact winding computation at level $105$ has total
coefficient-field weighted dimension $289=g(X_1(105))$.
Its unique deficient even-character orbit has order $6$ and
conductor $21$. Since $22\equiv1\pmod{21}$,
$(\langle22\rangle-1)J_1(105)(\mathbb Q)$ is finite.
The trivial-character component has dimension $13$ and full
winding submodule. Thus $J_0(105)(\mathbb Q)$ has rank zero.

Hasse's bound excludes order $105$ over every $\mathbb F_{2^e}$
with $1\leq e\leq8$, as also checked by the complete finite-field
enumeration. Hence an octic trace divisor $D$ has entirely
cuspidal reduction at $2$.
Lift it to a cuspidal divisor $C$ of degree $8$ over
the dyadically unramified field $\mathbb Q(\zeta_{105})$.
The finite-image assertion and Manin--Drinfeld show that
$y=(\langle22\rangle-1)[D-C]$ is torsion.
It reduces to zero, so Parent's lemma gives $2y=0$.
The resulting principal divisor has positive degree at most $32$,
whereas
\[
 \operatorname{gon}_{\mathbb C}X_1(105)
 \geq\frac{325\cdot4608}{32768}
 =\frac{2925}{64}>32.
\]
Thus $\langle22\rangle D=D$.

This diamond has order $4$, and the minimum CM degree on
$X_1(105)$ is $96>8$. Its action on the support is therefore
free. Quotienting its orbits gives a rational effective divisor
of degree $2$ on $X_1(105)/\langle22\rangle$.
Push this divisor to $X_0(105)$ and denote the result by $D_0$.
It is noncuspidal, effective, rational, and of degree $2$.
For the forgetful map $\pi_0:X_1(105)\longrightarrow X_0(105)$,
we have $\pi_{0*}D=4D_0$.
Proper pushforward commutes with specialization of integral
zero-cycles. Since $\overline D$ is entirely cuspidal,
so is $\overline D_0$.
This uses good reduction of the two modular curves; no assertion
about good reduction of the intermediate order-$4$ quotient is needed.

The level $105$ is squarefree, so all eight cusps of $X_0(105)$
are rational and remain distinct at $2$.
Consequently $\overline D_0$ lifts to a rational cuspidal divisor
$C_0$ of degree $2$.
The rank-zero assertion implies that $[D_0-C_0]$ is rational
torsion, and Parent's lemma gives $2[D_0-C_0]=0$.
However, Najman--Orli\'c, \emph{Gonality of the modular curve
$X_0(N)$}, Proposition~5.18, gives
$\operatorname{gon}_{\mathbb C}X_0(105)\geq6>4$.
The principal divisor $2(D_0-C_0)$ must therefore be zero,
contradicting the noncuspidal support of $D_0$.
\end{proof}
