\begin{lemma}\label{lem:nonic45-injective-two}
Reduction at $2$ is injective on $J_1(45)(\mathbb Q)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By \cite[Theorems~3.1 and 4.13, and Table~2]{DEHMZ}, the rational Jacobian has
rank zero and $2$-primary torsion $C_4\times C_{32}$.
The automorphism group of this $2$-group is a $2$-group: the unequal
invariant exponents give a characteristic flag modulo $2$, on which
automorphisms are upper unitriangular, and the kernel is a $2$-group.
Thus $H=\{1,16,31\}$ acts trivially on this torsion by diamonds.
Put $Y=X_1(45)/H$, and let $\pi:X_1(45)\to Y$ be the quotient map.

Over $F=\mathbb Q(\zeta_3)$, the curve $Y$ is isomorphic to
$X_1(3,15)$, taking the connected component with fixed Weil pairing
$e_3(5Q,T)=\zeta_3$. Indeed quotient a pair $(E,P)$ of order $45$ by
$\langle15P\rangle$. The image of $P$ has order $15$, and the dual
kernel has a distinguished generator after fixing $\zeta_3$ and the
generator $15P$ of the original kernel, using the isogeny pairing.
The dual kernel is independent of the order-$3$ point in the image
of $\langle P\rangle$: the dual isogeny sends that point to the
nonzero point $15P$. Replacing $P$ by $16P$ leaves this structure
unchanged. Conversely, the three lifts of the order-$15$ point through
the dual isogeny form exactly this $H$-orbit. This identifies the
quotient generically, hence identifies the smooth projective curves.

All sixteen rational cusps of $X_1(3,15)_{\mathbb F_4}$ lift to
$F$-rational cusps. An independent Tate-label enumeration verifies
that the generator $7$ of $\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb Q(\zeta_{15})/F)$
and Frobenius $4$ have the same sixteen fixed geometric cusps;
their other orbit lengths are respectively $4^4$ and $2^8$.
Native finite class-group computations give
\[
 \operatorname{Cl}_{\mathrm{rat\ cusp}}(X_1(3,15)_{\mathbb F_4})
       =[3,3,24,96,96],
 \qquad J_1(3,15)(\mathbb F_7)=[3,3,3,24,480,480].
\]
Both have $2$-primary order $8192$.
The first is a lower bound for the reduction of the $F$-rational
cuspidal subgroup, by Manin--Drinfeld. The second bounds the full
$F$-rational $2$-primary torsion from above, since $7$ splits in $F$
and prime-to-$7$ torsion injects into good reduction.
Consequently reduction at $2$ is injective on $J_Y(F)[2^\infty]$.
Both computations have been rerun in the degree-nine directory with
actual exit code $0$.

For $t\in J_1(45)(\mathbb Q)[2^\infty]$ we have
$\pi^*\pi_*t=\sum_{h\in H}ht=3t$, so $\pi_*$ is injective on this
$2$-group. The quotient and the $F$-isomorphism extend to the good
Neron models at $2$. If $t$ reduces to zero, so does $\pi_*t$, whence
$\pi_*t=0$, then $3t=0$ and $t=0$.
Odd-order torsion always injects under reduction at $2$, proving the lemma.
\end{proof}
