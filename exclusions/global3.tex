\begin{proposition}\label{prop:global3}
No elliptic curve over a decic field has a point of any order listed
in Table~\ref{tab:global3}.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Write $I_N=[\operatorname{PSL}_2(\mathbb Z):\overline{\Gamma_1(N)}]$.
The gonality bound of Abramovich with the Kim--Sarnak constant gives
$\operatorname{gon}_{\mathbb C}X_1(N)>325I_N/32768$.
The global Hecke criterion of \cite[Proposition~4.4]{DN}, at $q=3$,
applies when $10k_3<325I_N/32768$.
If $J_1(N)(\mathbb Q)$ is finite, take $k_3=4$; there is then no
noncuspidal effective rational divisor of degree $10$.
Otherwise take $k_3=7$ for $a=3$ or $3^{-1}$, and $k_3=8$ otherwise.

For every remaining row we verify that
$(\langle a\rangle-1)J_1(N)(\mathbb Q)$ is finite.
The complete newform lists include every oldform multiplicity;
every positive-rank character is trivial at $a$.
Alternatively, the complete exact winding computation has the full
genus as its weighted dimension sum, and every character component
without a full winding submodule is trivial at $a$.
Kato's theorem gives the required finiteness assertion.
Full-rank modular Krylov matrices certify many winding components;
partial Krylov spans are only diagnostics, and their characteristic-zero
winding computations are used instead. The independent replay distinguishes
these two cases.

The table verifies the degree-$10$ inequality, excludes CM points,
and gives diamond order not dividing $10$. A hypothetical trace divisor
must be diamond invariant by the global criterion. Every diamond orbit
in its non-CM support has the displayed length, which must divide $10$.
This is a contradiction. Each inequality has been recomputed for degree
$10$; no earlier-degree exclusion is transferred as such.
\end{proof}
