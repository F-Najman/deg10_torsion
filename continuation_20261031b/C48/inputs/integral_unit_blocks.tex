\begin{lemma}\label{lem:octic-mixed2-unit-blocks}
Let $N\geq5$ be even. On $X=X_1(2,N)$, write the universal
elliptic curve over the open modular curve as
\[
 E:y^2=x^3+cx^2+dx,\qquad P_2=(0,0),\qquad Q=(b,b),
 \qquad d=b(1-b-c).
\]
The functions
\[
 b,\quad d,\quad c^2-4d,\quad x(kQ)\quad(1\leq k\leq N/2)
\]
are units on the smooth integral modular curve over
$\mathbb Z[1/(2N)]$. At a prime $p\nmid2N$, group the closed
residue cusps by the vector of valuations of these functions.
The sum of the cusps in each group has a canonical lift to a
$\mathbb Q$-rational effective cuspidal divisor of the same degree.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Translate $P_2$ to $(0,0)$ and complete the square. The sections
$Q$ and $P_2$ are disjoint, and $Q$ is not a point of order $2$.
Thus $x(Q)$ and $y(Q)$ are units. The change of scale
$u=y(Q)/x(Q)$ is a unit and gives the displayed normalization.
Its uniqueness makes $b,c,d$ global functions on the open
modular curve. The discriminant is
$16d^2(c^2-4d)$, a unit there; hence $d$ and $c^2-4d$ are units.
The independence of $P_2$ and $Q$ implies
$kQ\notin\{O,P_2\}$ for $1\leq k\leq N/2$.
The divisor of $x$ on a smooth elliptic curve is
$2(P_2)-2(O)$, so each $x(kQ)$ is a unit as well.

The compactified modular curve has good reduction away from $2N$,
and its cusp scheme is finite \emph{\'etale} there. The divisors of
these units are therefore horizontal and supported on the cusps.
Their valuation vectors commute with reduction and are constant
on global Galois orbits. Each collection with a given vector is
Galois-stable; its reduced divisor is exactly the sum of the
closed residue cusps with that vector. This proves the assertion.
\end{proof}
